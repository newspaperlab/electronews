Microchip introduces ATFCD52 to read tactile signals for robot grippers

Acquisition reaches up to 800 Hz depending on array size. An external processor calculates forces and grip adjustments.

Newspaper Lab · Published 2026-11-11
Source announced 2026-11-10

Microchip announced the ATFCD52 capacitance digitizer on November 10. It converts changes from a fine-grid tactile sensor array into digital data. The company specifies up to 800 Hz depending on array size and sensitivity designed to resolve changes below one femtofarad. It lists the device as purchasable through Microchip or distributors; stock and delivery need regional confirmation.

The semiconductor and tool supplier provides a signal interface, rather than a complete robot hand. A camera locates an object, while contact information helps determine how hard a finger presses or whether it slips. Soft objects can be damaged by excessive grip or dropped by insufficient force.

Capacitance describes charge storage between electrodes. Deformation of sensor materials can change it. ATFCD52 digitizes the change, and an external processor can calculate normal and shear forces. Material, mechanics and calibration affect the result. Electrical sensitivity alone does not establish equal force accuracy with every material.

Counting channels and grid positions

The specification gives up to 52 channels, with up to 32 transmit and 20 receive lines. Their grid intersections make channel count different from sensing-position count. Twenty parallel analog front ends acquire receive lines simultaneously. An 800 Hz maximum corresponds to roughly 1.25 ms between frames. That is acquisition timing, not the total delay before the motor changes grip.

Microchip describes frequency hopping and oversampling to mitigate noise from motors and power electronics. Oversampling uses more frequent measurements to help handle noise. Communication, force calculation, commands and mechanics add further delay. Our interpretation is that improved acquisition is one control-loop stage; drop prevention requires system testing.

Match object surface and shape as well as weight during evaluation. Hard and soft surfaces can deform a sensor differently at equal force. Record actual slip alongside sensor patterns to assess controller decisions.

An EV-ATFCD52 board and configuration and logging software support evaluation of sensor materials and stack-ups. The release gives no hand-level success rate or force-error table for standard objects. Test sensitivity, array speed, processor timing and mechanical response together when evaluating adoption.
