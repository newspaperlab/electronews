Razer’s limited Raiju controller puts PC polling conditions in focus

The November 3 edition combines a 260g magnesium structure with features whose platform conditions matter.

Newspaper Lab · Published 2026-11-07
Source announced 2026-11-03

Razer announced Raiju V3 Pro Signature Edition on November 3, with an initial 1,337 individually numbered units, a magnesium-alloy structure and 260g weight. It is officially licensed for PS5, but the maximum 2,000Hz wireless polling specification applies on PC. Licensing and a PC performance figure cover different things.

Materials versus feel

Raiju dates to 2016; this edition emphasizes construction and finish. Metal, glass and grip materials can affect touch, but do not establish faster human reactions. Published weight also does not decide comfort for every hand and grip. This is not a hands-on fatigue review.

Contactless TMR sticks sense magnetic changes electrically. The approach aims to avoid contact wear, but the sensor label does not guarantee no failures or input errors. Dead zones and sensitivity influence control, and game-specific settings still matter.

Polling versus complete latency

At 2,000 reports a second, the theoretical interval is 0.5ms. That is not the entire button-to-screen delay: game processing, rendering and display follow. Razer specifies HyperPolling Wireless on PC, not an identical PS5 guarantee. Its footnote says PC compatibility has not been tested or endorsed by Sony Interactive Entertainment.

Six remappable multifunction buttons, alternative stick caps and back-button covers extend customization. Extra inputs can keep thumbs on the sticks, but require learning and differ across games. Check platform, app and game rules when storing profiles; more parts do not ensure better control for everyone.

The product page lists Razer.com and selected RazerStores, while Korean price, stock and delivery are not established by the release. Limited supply does not guarantee resale appreciation. Compare required inputs, platform support, warranty and total cost. The overlap between performance peripheral and collectible is an interpretation of the package, not a prediction of satisfaction or competitive results.
