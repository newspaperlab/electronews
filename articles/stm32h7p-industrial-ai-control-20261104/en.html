STM32H7P adds signal processing and AI acceleration to industrial control

Production is planned for January 2027; CoreMark and GOPS measure different workloads.

Newspaper Lab · Published 2026-11-12
Source announced 2026-11-04

STMicroelectronics announced STM32H7P microcontrollers on November 4, combining an Arm Cortex-M7 with its HSP signal processor and Neural-ART NPU for motors, power control and monitoring. OEM sampling is underway, with production planned for January 2027. Evaluation and Discovery kits are also scheduled for that month. Announcement is not immediate general retail availability.

ST supplies chips across industrial, automotive and consumer markets. STM32 is its MCU family; H7P extends the high-performance H7 range. MCUs read inputs and produce control outputs inside a device. Adding signal processing and small-model inference enables local decisions rather than sending every measurement to a server.

The release lists 2773 CoreMark for the Cortex-M7 and up to 85 GOPS for the NPU. CoreMark is a score from defined processor-test code; GOPS counts billions of operations per second. They describe different work and hardware, so cannot be added or translated directly into PC responsiveness. Model structure, data format and memory transfers affect real inference latency.

ST’s speed comparisons are model-specific: it reports 40-times-faster inference than software for its solar-inverter arc-fault model, 20 times for anomaly detection and 15 times for battery management. These are manufacturer comparisons, not universal gains for every network or complete device. Faster detection of abnormal electrical arcing does not replace validation of the full protection system.

The range offers up to 2 MB of flash and larger tightly coupled memories. Flash retains code and data without power; TCM provides closely connected, predictable fast access for processing. Capacity and access behavior are distinct. ST describes pricing similar to earlier 1 MB H7 products, but package and order quantity must match in a meaningful comparison.

The release points to Edge AI Suite, motor-control tools and an updated STM32Cube environment. ADCs convert analog inputs to digital values; DACs do the reverse, while comparators and timers also support control. Improved peripherals do not prove drop-in pin or software compatibility. Inputs, rates, memory placement and thermal behavior remain design checks.

The stated starting price is US$4.43 for a 1 MB flash device in LQFP100 at 10,000-unit orders, not a development-kit or single-unit retail price. Ambient operation up to 125°C is not unconditional safety for an entire system. Our interpretation is that local control-plus-inference options are broadening; regional stock, final prices and real model latency still need verification.
