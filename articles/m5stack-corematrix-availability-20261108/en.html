M5Stack lists CoreMatrix, a compact 256-LED IoT controller

The official listing shows $29.90 and ESP32-C61 hardware; its original launch date is not disclosed.

Newspaper Lab · Published 2026-11-08
Listing checked 2026-11-08

M5Stack's official store showed CoreMatrix available to order for $29.90 when checked on November 8. It combines an ESP32-C61, 16×16 white LED matrix, motion sensing, buttons and wireless connectivity. The listing does not disclose an original launch date, so November 8 is a check date, not a claimed release date. Price and stock may change.

A programmable status panel

M5Stack makes modular development hardware. This is an IoT controller users program, not a fixed-purpose finished app. Sixteen by sixteen gives 256 display points, useful for icons and feedback rather than photographs or long text. The actual behavior depends on software.

Specifications include a 160MHz single-core RISC-V processor, 16MB flash and 8MB PSRAM. Flash retains programs and data; PSRAM provides working memory. They should not be added as interchangeable storage. Clock frequency alone is not a cross-device performance benchmark. Scheduling display, sensing and transmission affects response.

Wireless and motion

2.4GHz Wi-Fi 6 does not establish 5GHz or 6GHz support. Check router bands and interference. The BMI270 six-axis IMU measures acceleration and rotation for motion-based interaction, not automatic map positioning. Applications still need processing and calibration; a component name is not a finished feature.

microSD, buzzer, three user buttons and expansion interfaces are included. The package lists the device and a hex key, not every external sensor or battery. Check module connectors, power and software. USB-C does not make every accessory interchangeable. Start with one intended behavior and verify documentation and examples before multiplying parts.

The listed price excludes shipping calculated at checkout and is not a complete landed cost. CoreMatrix offers an integrated status-display option, but does not finish an existing project just by being plugged in. That is a hardware interpretation, not a claim of hands-on testing. Keep the unknown launch date separate from the verified sale listing, then check documents, stock and final cost before ordering.
