Sony RIALTO 65 adds a larger-sensor option to VENICE 2, planned for 2027

Its sensor area is about 2.2 times 35mm full frame. The 65mm format name is not the length of one sensor edge.

Newspaper Lab · Published 2026-11-09
Source announced 2026-10-29

Sony's October 29 RIALTO 65 announcement describes a larger-sensor extension system for existing VENICE 2 cinema cameras. US availability is tentatively the first half of 2027, at an announced US$80,000 MSRP. This is a future-product announcement, not confirmation of sales starting now. The key change is access to a larger imaging area while retaining the camera body.

VENICE 2 belongs to Sony's CineAlta professional cinema range used for productions such as films and advertising. RIALTO 65 is not an independently usable consumer-camera accessory. Its sensor head connects to VENICE 2, whose body handles primary functions including recording. Dedicated firmware version 6.00 or later is required. Attachment to existing equipment does not establish immediate compatibility with every current setup.

Look beyond the format name

The sensor measures 53.75 by 35.83mm, with an approximately 64.60mm diagonal. The 65mm designation comes from a cinema-film format and describes an imaging-area class, not an edge measuring exactly 65mm. Its area is about 2.2 times that of 35mm full frame. That is an area comparison, not a promise of 2.2 times the resolution or sensitivity.

Announced readout modes include 9.6K 1.5:1 Open Gate and 9.6K 2.20:1. Open Gate uses a broad sensor area to preserve framing choices for post-production. Results still depend on lens coverage, aspect ratio and the finishing workflow. A larger sensor alone does not guarantee a particular cinematic appearance or depth of field.

Separate the head, retain the workflow

Extension Mode separates the head from the body by cable; Studio Mode attaches it directly. This increases placement choices where a full camera body is difficult to position. Recording uses the established VENICE 2 X-OCN and AXS-media workflow. Our interpretation is that preserving some existing equipment can help adoption of the larger format, without eliminating every additional cost.

The system does not include a lens mount and requires a third-party adapter. Lens coverage of the larger image area also needs checking. The $80,000 figure is the US announced MSRP, not a regional tax-inclusive quote or a complete production-system budget. Optional accessories, including a longer cable, are separate. Owning the camera body alone is insufficient to calculate the final installation cost.

Sony says final specifications may differ and further readout modes are planned through software updates. The US release does not establish a Korean launch date or price. Production teams should assess firmware, lens compatibility, recording and post-production conditions alongside sensor size and resolution. This report analyzes published information; it is not a hands-on shooting or image-quality test.
