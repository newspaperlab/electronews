A 12-meter Samsung Onyx goes into a Scottsdale cinema

RoadHouse installed a direct-view LED screen in auditorium 10. Film formats still depend on the screening.

Newspaper Lab · Published 2026-11-13
Source announced 2026-11-12

Samsung’s US division announced on November 12 that a 12-meter Onyx screen had been installed in auditorium 10 at RoadHouse Cinemas in Scottsdale, Arizona. It calls this Arizona’s first Cinema LED screen. The project covers one auditorium. The release confirms installation, without giving the exact public opening date or first film schedule.

Onyx is Samsung’s direct-view cinema LED line. A projector sends light onto a reflective screen. In a direct-view system the LEDs themselves emit light, allowing dark areas and small bright highlights to be handled separately. Exit lighting, doors and reflections can still affect perceived blacks.

Samsung cites a 300-nit HDR peak and compares it with a 48-nit conventional cinema reference. Nits measure luminance. Dividing the numbers gives 6.25, a ratio of quoted values rather than an increase across every frame. HDR extends the presentation range between shadows and highlights, with actual presentation depending on the available content format.

The display supports 4K and 120 frames per second. The release describes up to 4K 96 Hz with the GDC SR-5520 server used in this installation. Display capability, server output and a movie’s distributed frame rate are separate limits. A higher display ceiling does not automatically add frames to an existing movie.

DCI certification concerns technical requirements for digital cinema. The media server, display and audio system serve different jobs. RoadHouse also added DTS:X and heated recliners. Check whether a screening is in auditorium 10. Samsung says the format carries no surcharge in its first operating month, but provides no exact starting date.

RoadHouse’s Scottsdale page also presents Onyx as coming soon. Installation and programming a public screening are different milestones. A ticket buys a particular showing, even when the same film plays in multiple formats. Check the auditorium identifier, and use an operator listing to confirm the first public date once available.

We see the installation as a case for comparing what equipment and formats a screening uses. Brightness and contrast are manufacturer specifications, with no independent measurements of seating positions or sustained output in the cited release. Auditorium, format and ticket price need checking for the selected showing.
