Sony revives a Walkman-era idea with USB-C MDR-3R headphones

European sales are planned for November. A simple wired connection still requires compatible USB audio.

Newspaper Lab · Published 2026-11-06
Source announced 2026-11-05

Sony announced the USB-C wired MDR-3R on November 5. It revisits the idea behind the MDR-3 introduced with the first Walkman in 1979, adding a modern connector, call microphone and inline remote. European availability is scheduled for November at a suggested €59 or £49. That does not establish the same price or immediate availability in every country.

Less connection management

Sony has a long history in players and headphones. This model offers a different routine from true-wireless earbuds and their charging cases: plug into a supported device without Bluetooth pairing or headphone charging. It still uses power through the connection, occupies the device's port and introduces a cable. Those practical constraints matter alongside the simpler setup.

Sony says 23mm drivers, matching the original size, are tuned toward the earlier sound. A driver converts electrical signals to sound; diameter alone cannot rank sound quality against another headphone. Mounting, tuning and fit also matter. The claimed resemblance describes the manufacturer's intention, not a listening comparison conducted for this article.

Connector shape is not full compatibility

The stated requirement is USB Audio Class 1.0 support. USB-C describes a connector, not identical behavior on every device. Sony cautions that settings may be needed and some functions can vary with hardware, operating system and app. Buyers planning calls and remote control should check those functions, not just playback. A Walkman-inspired design does not imply direct compatibility with older players.

The lightweight open-ear structure and interchangeable blue and orange pads emphasize a particular fit. An open structure has different isolation characteristics from a sealed design. Quiet-room comfort and listening on noisy transport are different tests. People prioritizing noise reduction should separate styling from functional requirements; a manufacturer's comfort description cannot determine everyone's fit.

The simultaneous Stranger Things edition is priced at €69 or £59. Its collaboration design and packaging may appeal, but they are not evidence of improved sound. Exact local stock, country-specific launch dates and Korean pricing are not established by this European release. The significance is a wired option that reduces pairing and charging management, an interpretation of the disclosed features rather than a hands-on recommendation.
